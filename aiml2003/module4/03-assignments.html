Deliverable 1: Module 4 Lab Lab

Overview

In the readings and in class, you learned how RAG combines retrieval with generation: relevant passages are pulled from a document, injected into a prompt, and used by the LLM to produce an answer grounded in evidence. This lab gives you a working RAG pipeline — PDF text extraction, chunking, embedding with Nomic Embed, ChromaDB storage, and Gemini-powered answer generation — and asks you to investigate how it works. You run the system in Part 1, then probe the text vector space through four experiments: the similarity game, embedding surgery, the caption bridge, and a chunking strategy showdown.

The markdown cells you write after each experiment are the core of this lab. They’re where you explain what you observed, what surprised you, and what it means. Those reflections are also the substance of your demo.

What to Submit

  • A completed Colab notebook (.ipynb) saved to your GitHub repo at module4/lab4-nlp-investigation.ipynb.
  • All cells executed with visible outputs (don’t clear outputs).
  • Markdown reflection cells completed for every experiment.
  • No API keys or secrets anywhere in the notebook.
  • Submit the GitHub link in Canvas. If it’s not in the repo, it doesn’t exist for grading.
Start this in Thursday lab. We’ll run through Part 1 together and start the experiments. Bring a PDF you want to use — at least 10 pages of selectable text. If you don’t have one, grab a book from Project Gutenberg. Come with the readings done so you know what RAG is before we investigate one.

Deliverable 2: Module 4 Demo Demo

Overview

A three- to five-minute live demo during Tuesday’s class (April 21, 5:30 PM). Your notebook is your demo — no separate slides required. Walk the class through one experiment in depth, show a failure, demonstrate the RAG pipeline live, and share what you learned.

What to Cover

  1. One experiment in depth: Pick the experiment that surprised you most. Show the code, show the output, and explain what you learned. This is the centerpiece.
  2. A failure: Show something that didn’t work the way you expected — a bad query, a hallucination, a chunking result that hurt instead of helped. Explain why you think it failed.
  3. The RAG pipeline in action: Ask your document one question live. Show the retrieved passages and explain why the system found them.
  4. One sentence you couldn’t have said before this lab: What do you now understand about embeddings, vector search, or chunking that you didn’t before?

Format

Share your screen and run your notebook live. If you attend via Zoom, have your notebook open and ready to run before your turn.
