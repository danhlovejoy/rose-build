Combined Final Reflection

AIML 2003 + AIML 2013: For Dual-Enrolled Students

Due: Thursday, May 14 by 11:59 PM. Submit in Canvas (AIML 2003).

Overview

You took both courses at the same time. You've seen language and vision from the inside, built projects in both, and watched the two fields collide in your combined labs. This reflection is your chance to write about what that was actually like — not what the syllabus says you learned, but what you noticed, what confused you, and what stuck.

500 words minimum. Submit in Canvas (AIML 2003). Like every assignment, you can use GenAI. But you have the tools to ensure it doesn't sound like GenAI. Be sure it doesn't.

Choose Your Prompts

Pick one or two of the following prompts. Answer in a single document.

Prompt A: Same math, different worlds

Vectors showed up everywhere this semester. You used them to represent words and to represent images. You computed similarity between sentences and between photographs. You watched a model turn a paragraph into a 768-dimensional point and a picture into a 2048-dimensional point. Pick one moment from each course where vectors clicked for you, or where they didn't. Were there times when the math was identical but the intuition felt completely different? What does "similarity" mean when you're comparing text versus when you're comparing faces?

Prompt B: The combined lab that taught you the most

You did combined labs that mixed NLP and CV in a single notebook. Pick the one that surprised you. What happened when you put language and vision in the same pipeline? Was there a moment where a technique from one domain helped you understand something in the other? Or a moment where they clashed? Where what worked for text didn't work for images, or the other way around? What specifically did the combined format show you that you wouldn't have seen in either course alone?

Prompt C: The thing you're still thinking about

Between the two courses, you've touched tokenization, embeddings, RAG, agents, edge detection, feature extraction, object detection, model evaluation, bias auditing, and image captioning.

Is there a question you didn't get to answer, a result you didn't fully understand, an ethical issue that didn't have a clean resolution? What is it? Why does it stick? If you had one more week with both courses running, what would you do with it?
