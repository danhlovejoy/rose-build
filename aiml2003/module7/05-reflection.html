Final Reflection

AIML 2003: Introduction to Natural Language Processing

Due: Thursday, May 14 by 11:59 PM. Submit in Canvas.

Overview

You write this after the presentations are done, when you've had a chance to hear what your classmates built and to sit with what you learned. Write about what happened when you were the one sitting at the keyboard: the specific moments, failures, and surprises that only you experienced.

500 words minimum. Like every assignment, you can use GenAI. But you have the tools to ensure it doesn't sound like GenAI. Be sure it doesn't.

Choose Your Prompts

Pick one or two of the following prompts. Answer in a single document.

Prompt A: The moment it stopped being magic

At some point this semester, something that felt like magic became something you could explain. Maybe it was the first time you saw a tokenizer split a word into subwords and it made sense. Maybe it was when you realized a "smart" chatbot was predicting the next token. What were you working on? What changed in how you thought about language models before and after that moment?

Prompt B: The project that fought back

Pick the lab where things went wrong. Your first approach failed, the output was garbage, you got stuck for an hour on a one-line fix. Walk me through it. What did you try? What finally worked? If you could go back to the version of yourself who was stuck, what would you tell them?

Prompt C: Your AI apprenticeship

You've been using GenAI tools to help write code all semester. Think about the first time you prompted one in Week 1 versus the last time. How did your prompts change? How did your trust change? Be specific: quote an early prompt and a recent one, or describe a time the tool gave you something wrong and how you caught it.

Reflection Rubric (100 points)

CriterionPointsWhat I'm looking for
Specificity 40 You name particular labs, notebooks, moments, and results from your own experience. I can tell you were in this class because nobody else could have written this.
Honesty & self-awareness 30 You say what you actually think, not what sounds good. Confusion, frustration, and unresolved questions count. Polished answers with no rough edges don't.
Depth of reflection 20 You go beyond describing what happened and explain what it meant to you. You connect your experience to how you think about NLP now.
Writing quality 10 Clear, readable, says what it means. Not graded on grammar or polish. Graded on whether I can follow your thinking.
AI tells will cost you points. You've spent the semester learning how these models write. You know what their output sounds like. Make sure patterns from the No Slop Repo don't show up in your writing. If I see them, I'll take off points for honesty and specificity. You have the tools to make sure this doesn't happen.
