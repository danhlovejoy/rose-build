Final Reflection

AIML 2013: Introduction to Computer Vision

Due: Thursday, May 14 by 11:59 PM. Submit in Canvas.

Overview

You write this after the presentations are done, when you've had a chance to hear what your classmates built and to sit with what you learned. Write about what happened when you were the one sitting at the keyboard: the specific moments, failures, and surprises that only you experienced.

500 words minimum. Like every assignment, you can use GenAI. But you have the tools to ensure it doesn't sound like GenAI. Be sure it doesn't.

Choose Your Prompts

Pick one or two of the following prompts. Answer in a single document.

Prompt A: What does a machine actually see?

In Week 2 you were looking at edge maps and HOG descriptors. By Week 6 you were feeding images into models that generate captions in English. Somewhere in that arc, your understanding of "what the computer sees" changed. When did the shift happen? Were you looking at a feature map, a confusion matrix, a set of bounding boxes? What did you think computer vision was before this class, and what do you think it is now?

Prompt B: The image that broke your pipeline

Pick the lab where your model or your code didn't do what you expected. Maybe your classifier was confident and wrong. Maybe your edge detector picked up noise instead of structure. Walk me through what happened. What did the failure look like on screen? How did you diagnose it? What did you learn about the gap between what a model produces and what a human would see?

Prompt C: Numbers you didn't trust

You've calculated accuracy, precision, recall, and IoU this semester. Think about a time when the metrics said one thing and your eyes said another. A model with high accuracy that was clearly failing, or a low score on a result that looked fine. Which metric was lying, and why? If someone handed you a confusion matrix right now and said "this model is 94% accurate," what's the first thing you'd check?
